Kilimanjaro Safaris

Disney's Animal Kingdom, Florida · Photo Tour #26

I rode Kilimanjaro Safaris at Disney's Animal Kingdom, the safari that's been running since the park opened on April 22, 1998. I started in line while a black-crowned crane checked our passports, then rode through the forest, the river valley, and the savannas past okapi, black rhinos, hippos, painted dogs, elephants, and lions.

Jumbo, everyone! For Photo Tour #26, I rode Kilimanjaro Safaris at Disney's Animal Kingdom. The safari has been one of the park's greatest attractions since the park opened on April 22, 1998, especially for animal lovers.

We started the safari in line before getting on the bus, because a black-crowned crane wanted to check our passports. As we hopped on the bus, we headed to the Little Ituri Forest, where animals blend into their surroundings, including the rare okapi, black rhinos (South-Central and Eastern black rhinos included), along with herds of eastern bongos and greater kudus.

Next is the Safi River Valley, where the giants of the rivers appear. One of them is the common hippopotamus, mixed with pink-backed pelicans. Up the river, you will also find Nile crocodiles just trying to get some sun.

Next is the Western Savanna, where African painted dogs hunt on the plains. They keep an eye out for the other animals, such as springbok, eland, zebras, and a growing herd of Masai giraffes. As we head back into the forest, you can try to find mandrills, but really it is the herd of African bush elephants that you should see the most, in one of the best African elephant habitats in any AZA zoo.

Our next stop was a Mickey-shaped lagoon for greater flamingos. As we moved on to the Eastern Savanna, you can see ostriches, waterbuck, and a herd of southern white rhinos, but I was just informed that predators were spotted, like the cheetah. The next hunter lives on a kopje, or in this case Pride Rock, for African lions.

The last residents on the safari are a mud pit for warthogs and the Wardens Outpost, which was taken over by Nigerian dwarf goats. After them, the safari has sadly come to an end, but Africa still continues with the nearby Gorilla Falls Exploration Trail.
