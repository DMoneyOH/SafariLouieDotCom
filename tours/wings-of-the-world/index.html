Wings Of The World

Cincinnati Zoo & Botanical Garden · Photo Tour #13

This building was opened back in 1936 as the zoo's reptile house, but as time moved on, the reptiles moved to the old monkey house, and the building was transformed into a bird house. It was later renovated in 1996 — and that's how Wings of the World was born.

The tour begins with the walk-through South American aviary — sunbittern, scarlet ibis, Inca terns, wattled jacanas, tanagers and boat-billed herons, and a pool for the mata-mata turtle. Next, the Oceania aviary: Victoria crowned pigeon, masked lapwings, shama thrushes, and the critically endangered Guam rail, once extinct in the wild and reclassified in 2019 thanks to conservation.

Then the mountain forest, now home to a tawny frogmouth, the African savanna with its golden-breasted starlings, and a beautiful keel-billed toucan where the rhinoceros hornbills used to be. The last section holds puffins, murres and guillemots, and an enclosure for three penguin species — king, rockhopper and Magellanic. Outside, look for one of the largest flocks of kea parrots in North America. Even though it's now called Birds of the World, Wings of the World has a special name for me — one of my favourite bird houses to visit.
